# Workflow: from goals to approved mockups (steps 1 to 7 of 10)

Tell the user the plan: "We plan, write and mock up first. Nothing goes live until you've reviewed a preview link and said yes." At each step, say "Step N of 10", what done looks like, and what comes next. Save AI drafts with a `-Claude` suffix (for example `homepage-copy-v2-Claude.md`) and never overwrite an earlier version. Update `HANDOFF.md` at the end of every session.

## Step 1 of 10: goals and audiences
Write a one-page brief from the profile: audiences, the top tasks for each, and what success looks like three months after launch. Done: the approver agrees the brief is right. Why: every later choice is checked against it.

## Step 2 of 10: inventory the current site
Walk the public site (browsing, `/sitemap.xml`, or a page list the user pastes or exports). Build `site-inventory-Claude.csv`: current URL, title, purpose, keep/merge/drop, new URL, redirect needed, forms on the page, embeds, notes. For each form, record its fields and the inbox submissions go to, never its responses. Note whether a privacy notice page exists. Flag stale content (past events, former staff, wrong times). Done: every page, file and form is listed. Why: forgotten pages become broken links after launch.

## Step 3 of 10: voice and protected wording
Summarize the voice in a short table: what it sounds like, what to avoid. Confirm the "Never change without asking" list in `HANDOFF.md` (mission statement, statement of faith, name). Done: the approver confirms the list and the exact text. Why: in real use, a draft quietly reworded a mission statement; the leader caught it and it was reverted. Protected text is quoted exactly from here on.

## Step 4 of 10: content plan and page list
Propose a short launch page list. Many churches need 6 to 10 pages: Home, Plan a visit, About and beliefs, Ministries, Events, Sermons, Give, Contact. For each page: purpose, audience, key content, next step for the visitor, source material. Map every old URL to a new page or redirect. Put other ideas on a "later" list. Done: the approver signs off on the list. Why: a small first launch actually ships.

## Step 5 of 10: tagline and homepage options
Offer three tagline options, each with why it works, who it lands with, and the risk. Then two or three homepage openings written as real copy (lead with the tagline, lead with the need you meet, lead with a welcome) with a one-line reason each. Recommend one and say why; the approver chooses. Test every line: "Could this appear on any church's site? If yes, sharpen it." Put options in a review sheet (a spreadsheet or table with option, decision and comment columns), then work in every comment and report what changed. Done: one tagline and one homepage direction chosen and recorded in the profile. Why: choosing between real options is faster and fairer than reacting to one draft.

## Step 6 of 10: page copy
Draft the homepage first, then the rest. Use the voice notes. Write in short blocks with a clear next step on every page, so sections can be swapped. Mark unconfirmed facts as placeholders. Use Scripture only if the ministry does; ask which translation, quote only from verified text, and stay within that translation's quotation permissions (most publishers state them). Revise section by section rather than regenerating everything. Second-model check: before a person reviews it, ask a fresh chat or a second model to audit the copy against the brief, the voice table and the protected wording; grade it, fix anything below an A, and report what changed. Done: approver-approved copy for every launch page. Why: a reviewer who gets clean copy can focus on what only they know.

## Step 7 of 10: mockups
Make a clickable mockup of every launch page, on phone and desktop widths: an HTML file the user opens in a browser, or an artifact (an interactive page Claude creates) they can share with the team. In real use this was a dozen or more pages. Use the ministry's logo and colors if supplied; otherwise neutral placeholders marked as such. Use only images the ministry has rights to; photos of people need consent under its policy. Done: the approver has seen every page and given feedback. Why: people react to pictures far faster than to plans.

Next: step 8 in [launch and cutover](launch-checklist.md).

## Useful follow-up prompts
- "Show me the protected wording list and confirm none of it changed in this draft."
- "Rewrite the Plan a visit page for someone who has never been to church. Keep every fact."
- "Put the three tagline options in a review sheet with decision and comment columns."
- "Audit all page copy for anything that could be on any church's website, and sharpen it."
- "Which old URLs still have no redirect?"
- "Update HANDOFF.md with today's decisions and the next three steps."
