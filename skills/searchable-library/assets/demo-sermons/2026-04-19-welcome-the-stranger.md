# Welcome the Stranger

Fictional sample data. A short, made-up sermon excerpt for practice; not a real transcript. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version (public domain).

- Church: Cedar Hill Community Church
- Date: 2026-04-19
- Speaker: Associate Pastor Priya Nair
- Passage: Romans 12:9-13; Hebrews 13:1-2
- Series: Love in Action

Romans 12 gives a picture of what love looks like in a church family. Love without pretending. Hate what is evil and hold on to what is good. Be kind to one another, and honor each other more than yourselves.

Verse 13 ends with two short phrases: share with the saints who are in need, and be "given to hospitality." The word Paul uses carries the idea of love for strangers. Hospitality is not about a clean house or a perfect meal. It is making room for someone who is not yet part of the circle.

Hebrews 13:2 says, "Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares." The writer is not asking us to guess who is an angel. He is saying that we never know what God is doing when we open the door.

First Peter 4:9 adds that we should show hospitality without grumbling. That one is for me. Hospitality is often inconvenient, and it still matters.

Here is where it gets practical. When a new family walks in on a Sunday, the first five minutes decide whether they come back. Learn one name. Offer to sit together. Invite someone to lunch after the 11:00 service. Our welcome team is looking for people who are good at noticing, not people who are good at talking.
