# Rest for the Weary

Fictional sample data. A short, made-up sermon excerpt for practice; not a real transcript. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version (public domain).

- Church: Cedar Hill Community Church
- Date: 2026-03-15
- Speaker: Pastor Dana Reyes
- Passage: Matthew 11:25-30
- Series: Hope in Hard Places

Some of you came in this morning running on empty. You have been carrying work, family, caregiving, and maybe three ministry roles here at church. Our volunteers are stretched thin, and I know it, because I am one of the tired ones too.

Jesus says, "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." He is not speaking to people who have it all together. He is speaking to the exhausted.

Then he says something surprising: take my yoke upon you. A yoke is a tool for work, not for a nap. Jesus is not promising a life with nothing to carry. He is offering to carry it with us, and he says he is meek and lowly in heart. His yoke is easy and his burden is light.

God built rest into creation. The Sabbath command in Exodus 20:8-11 points back to God resting on the seventh day. And Jesus says in Mark 2:27 that the Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. Rest is a gift, not a reward you earn after everything is done.

So here is a practical step. If you serve in more than two roles, talk with a ministry leader this month about setting one down for a season. Saying no to a good thing can be a way of saying yes to Jesus. And Galatians 6:2 reminds us to bear one another's burdens, so no one should carry it all alone.
