# Mercies in the Morning

Fictional sample data. A short, made-up sermon excerpt for practice; not a real transcript. Scripture quotations are from the King James Version (public domain).

- Church: Cedar Hill Community Church
- Date: 2026-01-11
- Speaker: Pastor Dana Reyes
- Passage: Lamentations 3:19-26
- Series: Hope in Hard Places

Lamentations is a book of grief. It was written after the fall of Jerusalem, and it does not rush past the pain. The writer remembers affliction and bitterness, and says his soul is bowed down. If you have lost someone this year, you are allowed to say that out loud here.

Then, in the middle of the grief, the writer turns. He calls something to mind, and because of it he has hope. The Lord's mercies do not fail. "They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness." Notice that he does not say the loss was small. He says God's faithfulness is greater.

One of our members, who asked me not to share her name, told me that after her husband died she read Lamentations 3 every morning for a month. She said it did not take the sorrow away. It gave the sorrow somewhere to go.

Verse 24 says the Lord is my portion. When the things we counted on are gone, God himself is still ours. Verse 26 says it is good to wait quietly for the salvation of the Lord. Grief takes time, and waiting is not failure.

Psalm 30:5 says weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning. The night can be long. Our grief care group meets on the first Tuesday of each month, and no one has to talk until they are ready.
