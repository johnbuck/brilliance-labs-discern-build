# Event templates

Shapes, not scripts. Fill from the profile and the user's facts; mark gaps "[confirm]". Write in the ministry's voice. No invented speakers, quotes, testimonials or numbers.

## Starting checkpoint thresholds

Offer only when there are no past numbers, and say they are starting suggestions. Many ministry events fill late, so early thresholds are low. Measure paid (or committed) registrations against the target.

| When | Threshold | If missed |
|---|---|---|
| Registration opens (often 10 to 12 weeks out) | Page live, venue confirmed, invitations drafted | Fix before promoting |
| About 8 weeks out | 20 to 25 percent of target | RETHINK |
| About 4 weeks out | 50 to 60 percent of target, or break-even if higher | RETHINK or POSTPONE |
| About 2 weeks out | 80 to 90 percent of target; final counts to vendors | Scale down, POSTPONE or CANCEL |

Zero paid registrations at any checkpoint after the first is always a RETHINK, whatever the threshold.

## Event page and registration page

1. Headline: the promise in plain words ("You'll leave with ...").
2. Who it's for, in one or two lines, and who it isn't for.
3. What happens: 3 to 5 lines or a short schedule.
4. Date, time, place (or link), price, scholarships, childcare, accessibility, when registration closes, and the refund policy in one line.
5. Leaders, only confirmed names and roles.
6. One clear button or link to register, and what happens next (confirmation, the short survey).
Registration form: only the fields the event needs. Ask about access and dietary needs; don't ask for data you won't use.

## "Help me decide" guide

A short page or email for people who are unsure: "This is for you if ...", "It may not be for you if ...", which track or session to choose and why, what it costs in time and money, and who to ask.

## Invitation email and social posts

Email: subject line with the promise; a personal opening; who it's for; what they'll leave with; details; one link; signed by a named person the user confirms. Personal invitations from a trusted leader usually outperform broadcasts; offer a short version leaders can forward.
Social: three posts (announce, a reason to come, last call), each with the date and link. Drafts only.

## Pre-event survey (about 5 minutes)

1. Name and the email used to register (for matching). Or pass the registration ID in the link if the tool allows.
2. Where are you starting from? (3 plain options, from new to experienced.)
3. Rate these 5 to 8 topics or needs from 1 (not for me) to 10 (exactly what I need).
4. The one thing you most want help with, in your own words.
5. If this day went perfectly, what would you walk away with? (The outcome, not the method.)
6. Access, dietary or childcare needs.
7. Anything else the leaders should know.
If the event handles sensitive matters, add a line saying who will read the answers.

## Day-of run sheet

Columns: time, what happens, who leads, where, materials, backup plan. Include setup and teardown, a volunteer briefing, registration desk, first-aid and child-safety contacts (roles), and who can make decisions on the day.

## Feedback survey (about 2 minutes)

1. Overall, how useful was this? (1 to 10)
2. What was most useful?
3. What should we change?
4. What's one thing you'll do in the next two weeks because of today?
5. May we quote your answer, with or without your name?

## Recap

For leaders or the board: attendance against target, budget against actual, feedback scores and themes, what the survey changed, what to keep and change next time. Only real numbers; quotes only with permission.
