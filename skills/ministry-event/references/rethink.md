# Rethink: when registrations fall behind

Use when a checkpoint misses its threshold, or anytime the user worries the event isn't landing. Say plainly: a missed checkpoint is information, not failure. It is why the checkpoints exist.

A real lesson behind this step: an event with zero paid registrations nine weeks out was redesigned (shorter, a clearer promise, a different price and format) and then filled. Redesign early is often better than more promotion of the same thing.

## Step 1 of 5: read the numbers honestly
From the registration export: paid, free or comped, refunded, and the dates they came in. Compare with the threshold and with past events if known. Check the page and invitations actually went out, to whom, and when. Sometimes the problem is reach, not design. Name the venue's cancellation deadline and any money already committed: that is how long the team has to decide, and what a POSTPONE or CANCEL would cost today. Done: a one-paragraph read-out. Why: the numbers, the deadline and the money set the size of the decision.

## Step 2 of 5: ask the audience
Suggest the user ask 5 to 10 people who fit the audience, by conversation or a two-question message: "Would something like this help you? What would make it easy to say yes?" Draft the message; a person sends it. Listen for time, price, date, unclear value, or "not for me". Done: 5 to 10 answers, summarized. Why: the audience knows why it hasn't said yes; the team can only guess.

## Step 3 of 5: redesign the offer
Work through each lever with the user and show the options side by side:
- Promise: rewrite into one concrete outcome people want.
- Length: multi-day to one day, full day to half day, or a series of shorter sessions.
- Format: in person, online, hybrid, or hosted at partner churches.
- Price: lower price, early rate, group or church rate, scholarships, free with a suggested gift. Re-run break-even for each.
- Date: move if it collides with the audience's calendar.
- Invitations: from a trusted leader, personal rather than broadcast, and to partner churches or groups.
Mark every assumption. Recommend one option with reasons; the decider chooses. Done: the decider has chosen an option. Why: redesigning early costs less than more promotion of the same thing.

## Step 4 of 5: take care of people already registered
Draft a note that explains the change, honors their registration, and offers a choice (keep their spot, transfer, or refund any difference or the full amount, per the ministry's policy). A person sends it. Done: the note is approved and sent by a person. Why: how early registrants are treated is what they tell others.

## Step 5 of 5: relaunch and set a new checkpoint
Update the proposal and budget and get the approver's yes again. Redraft the page, the "help me decide" guide and invitations. Set a new checkpoint 2 to 3 weeks out with a threshold. Log everything in `HANDOFF.md`. Done: the relaunch is approved and the new checkpoint is dated. Why: a relaunch without a new threshold is only hoping.

If the new checkpoint also misses, lay out POSTPONE and CANCEL honestly, with costs already committed and what refunds would take. The decider decides.
