# Planning and running the event

Nine steps. Tell the user the step number, why it matters, and what done looks like. Keep the order: thresholds are set before promotion starts, and the survey shapes the day before the run sheet is final. Update `HANDOFF.md` at the end of each sitting.

Invite the current way of working: "Give me the goal, what you know, and any past files; I'll draft the plan and ask about gaps. Tell me what done looks like, and push back."

## Demonstration
Use [the Juniper Street brief](../assets/demo-juniper-street-event.md): run pass 1 (step 7 as of September 19, then [rethink](rethink.md)) and pass 2 (step 7 as of October 31, step 8 with the survey file, then the run sheet). Label everything "Fictional sample data". About 20 minutes. The brief's closing list says what a careful run should catch.

## Step 1 of 9: setup
Run [onboarding](onboarding.md). Done: profile and `HANDOFF.md` saved. Why: every later draft reuses these answers, and a teammate can pick up from the handoff file.

## Step 2 of 9: purpose and promise
Draft one purpose sentence (why the ministry is doing this) and one promise sentence for attendees ("You'll leave with ..."). Name 2 or 3 success measures (attendance, a follow-up step taken, feedback score). Done: the decider agrees with the promise. Why: a clear promise is what people register for, and every later draft repeats it.

## Step 3 of 9: format and schedule
Propose a format that fits the promise and audience: length, day and time, in person or online, sessions or tracks. Shorter often wins for busy people; ask what their audience can give. Draft a schedule with times and roles, gaps marked. If the team opens with prayer or worship, schedule it like any other item, with a name and a time. Done: a draft schedule the team can react to. Why: people say yes to a shape that fits their week, and every later draft describes this schedule.

## Step 4 of 9: budget, pricing and proposal
List costs from the user's numbers only, each marked quote, estimate or donated. Compute break-even: fixed costs divided by (price minus per-person cost). Show two or three pricing options (for example free with a suggested gift, a single price, an early rate plus scholarships) and what each needs in attendance to cover costs. Ticket income and gifts are recorded differently in the books and on donor receipts; before the page promises anything about tax deductibility, ask the treasurer or accountant. Draft a one-page proposal: purpose, promise, format, budget, pricing, checkpoints. Save it with a `-Claude` suffix.
Done: the budget approver has approved the proposal. Why: money and the ministry's name are committed from here on.

## Step 5 of 9: GO/NO-GO checkpoints
Set 3 or 4 dated checkpoints with numeric thresholds, before promotion begins. Why: a threshold chosen in advance is harder to explain away later. Use past events if known; otherwise offer the starting points in [event templates](event-templates.md) and let the decider adjust. Place the last hard checkpoint before the venue's cancellation deadline and the caterer's final-count date, so a POSTPONE or CANCEL still costs the least. Each checkpoint names the measure (paid registrations, total registrations, or break-even), the decider, and the options: GO, RETHINK, POSTPONE or CANCEL. Add them to the calendar only if the user asks and a calendar is connected. Done: the decider has agreed to the numbers in writing (in `HANDOFF.md` or the proposal).

## Step 6 of 9: pages, invitations and the "help me decide" guide
Draft from [event templates](event-templates.md): the event page, the registration page, a short "help me decide" guide (is this for me, which track or session), the confirmation message with the survey link, an invitation email, and 3 social posts. Second-model check: before a person reviews them, ask a fresh chat or a second model to audit the drafts against the promise and the audience, reading as a busy first-time attendee; grade each, fix anything below an A, and report what changed. Then a review round: a sheet with each draft, a decision column (OK, change, cut) and a comment column; work in every comment and report what changed. Why: five people's comments in one sheet get answered; in five emails they get lost. People publish and send. If the email platform is connected and the user asks, create drafts there and say exactly where; never send. Done: the team has approved every draft and knows who publishes what.

## Step 7 of 9: run each checkpoint
Ask for the registration export (only the columns needed: date, ticket type, status). Count against the threshold, show the pace, and note refunds and comps separately. If past events show late registration, say so. Recommend GO, RETHINK, POSTPONE or CANCEL with one paragraph of reasons; the decider decides. Why: a written recommendation makes the reasoning visible, so the decider can disagree with it. If the user's plan includes artifacts, a simple checkpoint dashboard helps the team see the trend. On RETHINK, go to [rethink](rethink.md). Done: the decision and its reasons are logged in `HANDOFF.md`.

## Step 8 of 9: pre-event survey, matched to registrations
Draft the survey from [event templates](event-templates.md) (about 5 minutes). Link it from the confirmation message. If the form tool can email each response to the organizer, suggest turning that on so leaders can prepare as answers arrive. When responses come in:
- Match each response to a registration by email or registration ID. Compare emails ignoring case and spaces. Report matched, responses with no registration, duplicates (keep the latest; flag it), and registrants with no response yet.
- Write a short readable summary per respondent for the leaders who will meet them, and a summary across everyone: top needs, ratings, starting points, access and dietary needs (counts only).
- Propose changes to the day: sessions to add, cut or reorder, groupings, materials. Show what changed and which answers led to it. The event lead approves.
Why: a day shaped around what registrants asked for is the difference between a good event and a remembered one. Done: the team knows who is coming and what they need.

## Step 9 of 9: the day and after
Draft the run sheet from [event templates](event-templates.md): time, what happens, who leads, where, materials, and a backup plan for the likely problems. Then the feedback survey and, after the event, a recap using only real numbers and permitted quotes. If QuickBooks Online is connected and the user asks, compare actual costs with the budget. Why: next year's thresholds come from this year's numbers. Offer to save what worked as a template for next time. Done: the recap is with the decider and the lessons are in `HANDOFF.md`.

## Useful follow-up prompts
- "Here's this week's registration export. Where are we against the checkpoint?"
- "Rewrite the registration page so the promise is clear in the first line."
- "Read our event page as a busy first-timer; grade it and fix anything below an A."
- "Match these survey responses to registrations and tell me who hasn't answered."
- "Turn the survey summary into three changes to the schedule and explain each."
- "Make the day-of run sheet printable, one page per role."
- "Draft a reminder email for people who registered but haven't done the survey."
