# Workbook and memo

## Workbook tabs

Works in Excel or Google Sheets. Keep inputs in one place and formulas everywhere else.

1. **Read me:** ministry, budget year, accounting basis, draft status, who reviewed, and a legend: confirmed, proposed, PLACEHOLDER.
2. **Assumptions:** ID, assumption, value, basis or source, who decides, status. Every number used elsewhere points here.
3. **Budget by line:** last year actual, this year budget, this year forecast, next year proposed, change from forecast (amount and percent), and the assumption IDs behind each line. Totals for income, expenses and the operating result.
4. **Monthly:** each line spread by its pattern (giving by the monthly share, most costs evenly, known lump payments in their month). Then cash: starting cash, in, out, ending cash, the cash floor, and "draw needed" (how much reserve money would keep ending cash at the floor, zero otherwise).
5. **Scenarios:** downside, base and stretch giving side by side: operating result before any draw, total draw needed, lowest month-end cash and its month, and the break-even giving level (giving at which the year balances).
6. **Decisions:** the items the board approves, worded as in the memo.
7. **Questions:** number, asked by, question, answer, what changed in the workbook, status.

Show the operating result before any draw. That is the true gap between what the ministry expects to receive and spend.

## One-page board memo

File: `YYYY Budget board memo DRAFT-Claude.docx` (or .md). One page. In this order:

1. **What the board is asked to approve.** Usually some of: the operating spending plan (total expenses, by line in the attached workbook); a fundraising goal, if the plan depends on one; a maximum reserve or endowment draw, to be taken only as needed to keep cash above the floor (a backstop, not a plan); the cash floor policy itself, if not already adopted; and any delegation, such as compensation set by a committee within the budgeted total. Use the ministry's own words and numbers; include only items that apply.
2. **Headlines:** a small table of income, expenses, operating result before any draw, draw modeled against the limit, and year-end cash, next to this year's forecast.
3. **Scenarios:** downside, base, stretch; name the break-even giving level.
4. **What changed and why:** three to five bullets.
5. **Placeholders and open items:** each compensation figure, each estimate to true up (for example starting cash after the year-end close), and each conditional project left out. Say who will decide and when.
6. **Suggested motion:** for the chair or secretary to finalize on the agenda.

Keep assumptions and approvals visibly separate. A board member should be able to see in ten seconds what their vote commits the ministry to.

If a draw would come from a donor-restricted endowment, add one line: the gift terms and state law may limit what can be drawn; the treasurer is confirming with a CPA or attorney.
