# Packet parts

Plain words, short sentences, no accounting shorthand the board wouldn't know. Explain an unavoidable term in a few words the first time ("unrealized gain: a rise in market value of investments still held, not cash"). Figures only from the reports and the treasurer.

A board that reads this well is doing what Proverbs 27:23 asks of a shepherd: knowing the condition of the flock. Write so every member can.

## 1. One-page summary (first page)

Four short paragraphs, in this order:

1. **What happened:** income, expenses and the net result for the period and year to date, against budget, and the one or two lines that explain most of the difference. Say whether it looks like timing or trend.
2. **Cash position:** cash available against the board's policy (for example a minimum balance). Separate money set aside by donors or by the board from money available to spend. If the policy calls for an action, such as a reserve draw, say so and who decides.
3. **Restricted funds:** each fund's balance and any gift received with conditions.
4. **What the board must decide:** a short list. If nothing, say "No decisions are needed this period."

Keep investment return (dividends, fees and market-value changes) on one line below the operating result. Market changes are not cash and should not blur the operating picture. If the board wants a single reserve measure, "months of operating expenses covered by available cash" (available cash divided by average monthly expenses) is common; show the arithmetic beside it.

## 2. Budget vs actual

The table from `bva.py`: budget, actual, variance, percent, flag and confirmed explanation. State how the budget column was built (month by month or spread evenly). Add a line explaining the sign convention.

## 3. Charts

The script's bar charts are enough: income and expenses, budget beside actual. Add a monthly cash trend only if the board asks for one.

## 4. Balance sheet summary

Assets, liabilities and net assets at period end and a comparison date, grouped into a few plain lines (cash, investments at market value, other).

## 5. Notes on preparation

The basis of accounting (cash, accrual or modified cash; say which items are on which basis, such as investments at market value), the date books were closed and reconciled through, who reviewed, and any correction to a prior period and why. If the basis is unclear or has changed, suggest the treasurer confirm it with a CPA before the packet goes out.

## 6. Appendix (spreadsheet)

Tabs: monthly Profit and Loss for the year, full budget vs actual by line, balance sheet detail, restricted funds, and the close verification log if the ministry keeps one. Use live formulas for totals when your tools can build XLSX; otherwise one CSV per tab.

## Decision-items addendum (when the board is voting)

One section per agenda item: what the board is asked to decide, why it is in front of them now, the background in plain words, options and their cost, and risks worth naming. Point to the agenda for the motion wording so only one version exists. Claude can suggest wording for the agenda; the board chair or secretary finalizes it.

## Treasurer review memo (optional)

If the treasurer reviewed several months of closes for this packet, include or attach their memo: what was closed, verification results, decisions that need sign-off, and items still open. The monthly-books-close skill has a template.
