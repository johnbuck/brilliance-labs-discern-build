# Contested Topics — Domain Sweeps and Neutral Voice

Discipline for topics where sources actively disagree: public controversies,
competing technologies with partisan coverage, scientific disputes, anything
where "both sides" is a live phrase. Load at step 1 (the sweep plan), step 5
(the voice), and step 6 (the verdict-word check). On an uncontested topic
this file stays closed.

## Plan the domain matrix before the first search

A real evidence sweep covers every outcome domain, not the top hits. At
scope, for contested or decision-grade topics, enumerate the domains up
front — typically: benefits or efficacy; harms, costs, and risks; long-term
consequences; per-subgroup splits where behavior differs; adjacent or
comparable populations; mechanistic or theoretical grounds; and official
positions (standards bodies, regulators, professional societies). The point
of planning: the second pass becomes verification rather than expansion.
Sweeps that discover whole domains late get sent back.

## Plan the terminology matrix with it

Enumerate the term variants before the first pass so the user never has to
name one you missed: the field's current term, older and superseded
terminology, artifact-specific names, singular and plural, hyphenated and
unhyphenated forms, phrase-match and keyword-match. Run them as one batched
sweep and log each variant's result — including the zeros — per
`source-access.md`'s clean-negative rule.

## Weigh evidence by its design

- Grade every load-bearing claim by the strength of its design: controlled
  experiment over observational study, observational over self-report,
  measurement over assertion; first-party measurement over vendor-reported
  numbers; a benchmark's own results table over a slide citing it. Say
  which design each finding rests on.
- Where an authority has formally graded a body of evidence, quote the
  grading verbatim with its citation — never paraphrase a grading into
  something softer or stronger.
- Never cite on the strength of a search snippet, an
  abstract-on-an-aggregator, or a writeup. Open the primary and read it;
  reading it yourself is the verification step.

## Map the debate, don't referee it

- Rebuttals, comments, errata, and same-venue exchanges between the stances
  are the concentrated documented argument — chase them deliberately with
  citation chaining; an erratum or comment record points to the original it
  corrects or answers. Record correction history; do not pick a side in it.
- Source each stance from its own flagship documents — position papers,
  regulator texts, primary statements — never from an opponent's summary or
  a caricature.
- Record methodological disputes as disputes on record. The report does not
  resolve them.
- Document the agreement zone too: what every stance's evidence supports is
  often the most usable output of the sweep.

## Claims-vs-evidence audit

For widely-asserted claims, deliver three sections: claims the corpus
supports; claims it contradicts or weakens; and claims in wide circulation
with no supporting research at all. The third section is the differentiator
— for each, say why it is unstudied (structurally unmeasurable, no outcome
studies, extrapolated from a different population). "No data exists" is a
finding; state it plainly.

## Voice discipline

- Strip verdicts from your voice: no bottom-line paragraphs, no evaluative
  adjectives (reassuring, strongest, compelling, solid, favorable), no
  sections where you adjudicate between studies or stances. Report counts
  and designs ("replicated across five cohorts") instead of quality
  verdicts ("well established").
- Keep findings in both directions, each with its design and limitation in
  the same cell. Stripping one direction to seem balanced is the bias.
- Quoted source wording is data; your adjective is opinion. Quotation marks
  around sources' own words — and none of yours doing the same job in the
  same sentence.
- Official positions land as a flat positions-as-published table: one row
  per body, identical format, one sentence noting the report does not
  adjudicate.
- Symmetry is mechanical, not aspirational: parallel rows per domain, the
  same format and citation density on both sides, and a symmetric section
  listing each stance's claims the corpus cannot support. Unequal list
  lengths are a finding — but only after sweeping both stances equally
  hard.

## Pre-delivery check

Grep your own draft for verdict words — reassuring, best-evidenced,
strongest, steepest, compelling, solid, favorable, defensible, sobering —
and their friends. Quoted wording stays; your voice goes. Run this as part
of the Final Pass on every contested-topic report.
