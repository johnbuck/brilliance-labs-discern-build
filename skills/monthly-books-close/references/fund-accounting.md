# Fund accounting basics, in plain language

Explain these when they come up, briefly, in the user's words. They are general US practice, not advice for a specific ministry. When Paul handled the gift for Jerusalem, he took care to do what was right "not only in the eyes of the Lord but also in the eyes of man" (2 Corinthians 8:21). A careful close is that same care.

## Words you'll meet

- **Bank feed:** the list of transactions QuickBooks Online pulls from the bank. Each line is matched to something already recorded or categorized to an account and class.
- **Journal entry:** a manual record for something the bank feed can't show, such as moving payroll cost between classes or updating an investment's value. Each has at least two lines.
- **Debit and credit:** the two sides of every entry. In every entry the debits and credits add up to the same total; that is what "balances" means. You don't need the theory: follow the close guide's pattern, and the checker confirms the totals match.
- **Reconcile:** compare QuickBooks with the statement until the difference is 0.00. It proves nothing is missing or doubled.
- **Clearing (or holding) account:** a temporary account money passes through, such as an online-giving payout before the gifts are recorded by fund. It should be zero at month end.
- **Class:** a tag on each line saying what the money was for. **Fund:** a pot of money set apart for a purpose, often tracked with a class.

## Restricted and unrestricted money

- **Without donor restrictions** (older term: unrestricted): gifts the ministry may use for any part of its mission.
- **With donor restrictions** (older terms: temporarily or permanently restricted): the donor set a purpose or a time, and the ministry accepted it. The money must be used that way. When it is spent for that purpose, it is "released" from restriction.
- **Board-designated** money (for example a building reserve the board set aside) is still unrestricted. The board can change its own designation; it cannot change a donor's.
- Whether a "designated" gift is legally restricted depends on what the donor was told and what they asked for. A giving page that says the ministry honors fund choices as preferences but keeps final discretion reads differently from a signed grant agreement. Have the ministry's gift policy and giving-page wording reviewed by the treasurer or a CPA.

## How restricted funds show up in QuickBooks Online

There is more than one sound way; pick one with the treasurer or CPA and keep it. Common choices: a class for each restricted fund; separate income accounts for designated gifts plus a monthly fund-balance schedule; or a fund-balance spreadsheet tied to QuickBooks totals. Whatever the method, each month answer: how much came in for each fund, how much was spent, and what is left.

## Classes

Classes tag each line by purpose. Many ministries use ministry areas (worship, youth, missions) plus administration and fundraising. Organizations that file Form 990 report expenses by program, management and general, and fundraising, so tagging every expense line each month saves a scramble at year end. A blank class on an expense line is a gap to fix, not a detail.

## Accounting basis, and what it means for investments

Cash basis records income when received and bills when paid. Accrual records them when earned or owed. Modified cash is cash basis with a few accrual-style items, most often investments carried at market value. The treasurer or CPA decides the basis; the board should record it, and the books, budget and board reports should all use the same one. On a modified-cash or accrual basis, the monthly unrealized gain or loss entry brings the investment account to the statement's market value; on a strict cash basis the treasurer may choose to leave investments at cost and skip that entry. Either way, market changes are not cash, and dividends reinvested inside the account are income even though no money reached checking.

## Gift rules worth knowing (United States)

These are the common federal rules as of 2026; check IRS Publication 1771 or a CPA for the current wording, and state rules may add to them.

- A donor needs a written acknowledgment from the ministry to deduct any single gift of 250 dollars or more. Flag gifts at that level for the person who sends acknowledgments, and confirm the ministry's year-end statement practice covers them.
- When a donor receives something in return for a payment over 75 dollars (a dinner, an event ticket), the acknowledgment must say what the goods or services were worth.
- A gift of stock, property or digital assets is recorded at its fair value on the day received. Ask the CPA before the first one.
- A minister's housing allowance must be designated in advance by the governing body. Payroll cannot fix it after the fact.

## When to call a CPA

Suggest a CPA (ideally one who serves churches and nonprofits) before posting when any of these appear: a correction to a prior year or a closed month; choosing or changing the accounting basis; how to present restricted funds; a first noncash gift; payroll tax notices or penalties; clergy tax questions; whether someone is a contractor or an employee; a grant with reporting conditions; whether an audit or review is required; anything that changes Form 990; or anything that looks like missing money. Say plainly that you can prepare the question and the supporting figures, but the answer is theirs.
