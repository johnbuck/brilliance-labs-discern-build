# Guided onboarding

## Opening

“I’ll help you connect your own Logos library, choose sources you can access, and produce a cited research packet. We’ll stop at research and exegesis. Are you on Mac or Windows, and do you normally use Logos on the web or desktop?”

If the environment or earlier answers establish this, confirm only what is missing. Explain that Linux/other environments can use source notes or available browser access, but no desktop integration is bundled. Do not claim OS-level browser automation has been tested on every platform.

## Save a workspace

Offer a clearly named local research folder. Run init with the user’s chosen label. Save OS and connection preference through preferences. This contains no login credentials. Keep each person’s sources in their own workspace. Do not create a global schedule or send anything.

## Connect and verify

Read connections.md. Explain the available route, open the actual Logos sign-in page only when using a browser tool, and let the user sign in themselves. Ask: “Please sign in in that window. Once you’re ready, we’ll check one book from your library.” Use tool-visible evidence to verify account/library and resource access, or label checks user-reported when relying on the user. If tools are unavailable, guide manual inspection and accept permitted source notes or exports. Do not report the connection as tested from a verbal sign-in claim alone.

## Research preferences

“What passage would you like to start with, and are there authors or commentary series you already trust?” Then ask: “What theological tradition, Bible translation, and language should guide the packet? How much depth would be useful?” Allow “no preference.” Save choices explicitly. Do not use Thomas’s personal defaults.

## Personal shelf

Inspect relevant accessible books in the library with the available tool, or ask the user to identify them through a guided review. Show a short proposed shelf with author/title, access evidence, relevance, and any limitation. Let the user adjust it. Register each verified resource using the helper. Do not imply that this guided step automatically scans the entire library.

“I found these sources available for your passage. Would you like to begin with these, or adjust the selection?” If none are accessible, report the gap and offer working from their own notes or another passage for which they have resources. Do not invent sources or require a purchase.

## First run and review

Read research.md. Start with one or two sources and a short passage. Record retrievals and coverage, create the packet, and show a summary next to its source. Ask: “Is this the right depth? Is there anything you want more of or less of next time?” Save adjustments. Report what was verified, what remains partial, and any unresolved connection issue.

Returning prompt: “Prepare a research packet for [passage] using my saved preferences and verified Logos resources. Stop at research and exegesis.”
