# Companion study resources

Create presentations, audio lessons, flashcards, and short descriptions that serve an existing lesson.
Keep each format faithful to its purpose rather than forcing one outline onto every resource.
Use user-approved document and audio tools; an optional source-notebook service may help.
No particular vendor, browser automation, connected account, or sibling skill is required.

## When to use

- Produce companion resources from an authorized chapter, guide, passage, or lecture.
- Resume production, check existing candidates, or describe finalized resources.
- Do not replace the controlling lesson or invent missing source content.

## Before you start

Confirm the lesson, audience, doctrinal frame, Bible translation, and requested formats.
Default to Berean Standard Bible (BSB) unless another translation is requested.
Ask which guide controls slides and which primary source controls audio.
Confirm required enrichment, branding preferences, intended access, and the final approver.
Ask what may be quoted, uploaded, processed externally, and distributed.
Keep licensed works, private answer keys, and personal details out of unauthorized uploads.
Do not assume ownership of a book grants permission to upload it to a service.
Choose the output folder and naming scheme with the user, not from another ministry's setup.

## Step 1: Confirm actual production capabilities

Identify tools that can read sources, create the requested files, and inspect their contents.
A document writer may create slides but cannot necessarily render them for review.
Audio production needs a working audio generator or approved recording workflow, not just a script.
A transcript needs access to the exact audio; an attachment alone does not prove content access.
Confirm supported formats, available generation capacity, export options, and any spending approval.
If a capability is unavailable, disclose it and offer an editable script or source file as a draft.
Never label a script as a completed podcast or a prompt as a finished presentation.
NotebookLM is optional; if chosen, verify its current capabilities and permitted source handling.
Do not upload materials or change account access merely because a tool is available.

## Step 2: Read and document the source packet

Read the full assigned primary materials and relevant enrichment in context.
Confirm the actual controlling guide; a publisher guide may differ from the leader's guide.
Do not invent an absent transcript or confuse a group discussion with a publisher lecture.
Keep a source manifest: title | role | version | locator | read status | permitted use.
Use the narrowest sufficient source packet and exclude unrelated reference works.
Where a service is used, verify each authorized source finished processing and avoid duplicates.
Converted sources must preserve required content and be labeled as derivatives.
Keep private preparation maps and answer keys private unless their use is explicitly approved.
Editorial codes and source-selection commentary must not appear in student resources.

## Step 3: Build a coverage ledger

Record each required guide topic, numbered statement, blank answer, question, and enrichment point.
For each item, list its source locator, intended artifact location, and final disposition.
Mark personal reflections and prayer plans ineligible for recall cards rather than inventing answers.
Map the primary chapter's flow separately for audio; do not assume guide order is chapter order.
Insert supplementary teaching where it genuinely clarifies the primary lesson.
Every required item needs coverage or an explicit, justified exclusion before completion.
If the guide and sources conflict materially, ask the leader instead of silently reconciling them.

## Step 4: Draft and generate each requested resource

Save the production instructions and keep user-approved wording intact unless changes are authorized.
Specify the audience, sources, required coverage, translation, privacy exclusions, and output format.
For external generation, verify the complete prompt and actual selected settings before submission.
Inspect existing artifacts first; do not duplicate a completed resource or mistake a slow run for failure.

### Presentation

Follow the controlling guide's sequence, teaching statements, and discussion locations.
Preserve required numbering and highlight completed blank answers when requested.
Allow multiple slides per section; do not merge distinct topics just to hit an arbitrary count.
Keep leader discussion cues distinct from model answers and private preparation notes.
Include playback cues only if the leader's actual meeting plan requires them.
Use readable type, restrained layout, and enough room for the complete required teaching.
Generate the requested presentation format, then render every slide for review.

### Audio lesson or podcast

Follow the primary chapter or passage's natural movement, not automatically the slide order.
Integrate required secondary teaching naturally without turning it into a second-book summary.
Use conversational attribution while distinguishing author teaching from editorial clarification.
Do not invent anecdotes, quotations, consensus, or promises of spiritual outcomes.
Generate actual audio through an approved tool, or deliver a clearly labeled recording script.
Preserve the completed audio and obtain a full transcript tied to that exact version.

### Flashcards

Use no more than 40 cards; fewer are preferred, and 40 is a ceiling rather than a target.
Each front must ask a clear, self-contained question with an objectively answerable back.
Each answer must state a concrete fact, definition, Scripture meaning, or definite taught principle.
Exclude subjective reflection, self-assessment, personal plans, and prayer-response prompts.
Never accept “answers vary,” “name a hurdle,” or “choose someone” as an answer.
A personal discussion prompt may inspire a different recall question about its taught principle.
Keep full required answers; split compound questions only when useful within the ceiling.
If essential eligible coverage cannot fit, report the conflict rather than exceeding the limit.
Export a portable front/back file or a verified artifact that does not expose the source packet.

## Step 5: Review complete candidates

Check every slide visually, every card front/back, and the full audio transcript against the ledger.
Verify every Scripture quotation, reference, and verse range using a reliable translation source.
The separately installed `bsb-scripture-retrieval` skill is optional; verified text access suffices.
Distinguish exact BSB quotations from faithful alternate translations and labeled paraphrases.
Check author quotations, source balance, required answers, discussion cues, and privacy exclusions.
Check transcript coverage against audio duration and investigate suspicious recognition errors.
Do not claim direct listening, pronunciation review, or visual review if only text was accessible.
Wrong Scripture, substantive doctrinal/source distortion, missing requirements, and unusability block readiness.
Harmless compression, repetition, or minor cosmetic flaws do not require regeneration.
Preserve the best candidate, target material corrections, and review the whole replacement.
Stop when complete review finds no blockers; report unresolved limitations rather than looping indefinitely.

## Step 6: Finalize files and descriptions

Obtain the leader's confirmation of final versions, including any manual edits, before descriptions.
Reopen those exact files; do not reuse an earlier candidate's review by filename alone.
Verify actual file types, nonzero content, opening/rendering, and playable audio where supported.
Draft descriptions locally from the finalized artifacts and verified transcript, not production prompts.
Describe the actual topic, teaching emphasis, and practical use in plain language.
Do not claim durations, counts, subjects, or features that have not been verified.
Do not expose private preparation or add ministry branding unless requested.
Check each description sentence against the corresponding final artifact.
A materially revised resource needs renewed review and description checking.

## Step 7: Deliver within the authorized scope

Deliver requested files, artifact-grounded descriptions, and a concise leader-only review note.
Identify final versus draft versions and state any unavailable generation or review capabilities.
Keep the source manifest, coverage ledger, prompts, and review notes separate from student copies.
Require explicit human approval before any publication, distribution, or access change.
Do not publicly share source notebooks or copyrighted source packets to make an artifact accessible.
If an artifact link exposes sources, use a permitted standalone export or report the access blocker.
For authorized distribution, set intended access explicitly and verify the exact saved file or link.
Read back the published target, description, and permissions before reporting success.
Creating resources alone does not authorize publishing; local delivery may be the complete task.
