# Policy outline and quick guide

A shape, not a script. Fill every section from the ministry's own answers and documents, in their voice. Leave a marked gap, not filler, where an answer is missing. Target 2 to 4 pages; cut before adding.

## The policy

Header: "[Ministry] guidelines for using AI", version and DRAFT label, date, policy owner (role), approving body.

1. **Why we have this policy.** The promise from step 2: what AI can free the ministry for, and that people and prayer stay at the center.
2. **What we believe.** 4 to 6 principles, each with its approved anchor (reference, plus quoted text only from the ministry's translation) and two or three sentences on what it means in practice.
3. **What AI is, and how it goes wrong.** Plain facts, a short paragraph each:
   - It produces likely text from patterns in what it was trained on; it does not know or discern the way a person does, and its confidence is not evidence it is right.
   - It can be confidently wrong, including about Scripture, dates and quotations.
   - It tends to agree with whoever is asking, so it can make a weak plan sound strong.
   - It carries biases from what it was trained on.
   - What you type may be stored, depending on the tool and its settings.
   - Tools that act (send, schedule, change records) can do more than asked, or report doing something they didn't.
4. **How we decide: our questions.** The 3 to 5 question filter, then a simple flow: pray and name the task; ask the questions; decide AI's role (none, helper, or does the work with a person approving); do the work with the review it requires; look back at what it produced and what it is forming in us.
5. **Where people stay involved.**
   - Human-only work (AI may help prepare or follow up, never stand in): the categories from step 5.
   - Reviewed before use by a named person: the categories from step 5.
   - Tools that act on their own (send, schedule, spend, delete, change records). The ministry decides how much freedom each tool gets and writes its own levels in its own words. Two or three are usually enough, for example from "a person does the work and AI assists", through "AI does the work and a person approves each action before it happens", to "AI runs on its own within limits we set". For any tool allowed to run on its own, offer questions to settle rather than conditions to copy: What could go wrong, and could it be undone? Could it contact anyone outside the team? What data could it see? Could it spend money, and up to what amount? Who owns it, how often do they look at what it did, and how do they turn it off? The ministry's answers become its conditions.
   - A short register of AI tools: tool, what it can see and do, its level, owner, how to stop it.
6. **Staff and volunteer guidelines.** Do and don't lists; which tools and accounts are approved (ministry-owned accounts over personal ones for ministry work); where to ask questions; training.
7. **Privacy and confidential information.** Data categories (public; internal; confidential, such as member records, giving, counseling and pastoral notes, health, personnel, anything about minors), and which tools may see each. Settings to check (for example whether the tool trains on what you enter). What to do if confidential data was shared by mistake.
8. **Disclosure.** When AI help is disclosed; that any AI that talks with the public says it is AI and hands off to a person for anything serious; the ministry's decision on AI-made voices or likenesses of real people.
9. **Tensions we hold.** Two or three in the leaders' words, with the question they ask when it arises. Examples to prompt them, not to copy: time saved versus time that was itself ministry; speed versus prayerful discernment; saving money versus the people whose work changes.
10. **Protecting our people (optional).** AI makes it easier to imitate a leader's voice, face or writing. Ask what the ministry already does to guard against impersonation and fraud, and what it wants to add; write down only what they choose. Questions to consider: How does staff confirm an unusual request for money, gift cards or account changes, even one that seems to come from the pastor? Who can approve a payment, and does anything need two people? What should someone do the moment they suspect a fake? Who speaks to the congregation if a leader's likeness is misused? The ministry's treasurer, bank and insurer may have their own requirements; suggest checking with them.
11. **Approval, review and versions.** Adopted by [body] on [date]. Reviewed every [month]. Version log table: version, date, what changed, approved by.

## The staff quick guide (one page)

- Title, version, "from our AI policy adopted [date]".
- The question filter, as printed.
- Good uses (3 to 5 examples from their own ministry).
- Never do (3 to 5 lines: confidential data into unapproved tools, AI in place of pastoral presence, publishing without review, and so on).
- Always: check facts and Scripture yourself; a person reviews anything in the ministry's name; disclose when the policy says to.
- Who to ask, by role.
