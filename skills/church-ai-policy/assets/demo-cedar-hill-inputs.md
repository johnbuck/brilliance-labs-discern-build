# Cedar Hill Community Church: AI policy inputs

**Fictional sample data.** Cedar Hill Community Church, its people and its history are invented for demonstration. Use these as if they were a leader's onboarding answers. Do not treat them as a real church's policy.

## Profile answers

- Ministry: Cedar Hill Community Church, a mid-size congregation (about 400 on a Sunday).
- Tradition: nondenominational evangelical; elder-led, with a congregational vote on budgets.
- Approving body: the Elder Board. Policy owner after approval: the executive pastor.
- Drafting team: Pastor Dana Reyes (lead pastor), Jordan Kim (executive pastor), Maria Santos (communications director).
- Data sensitivity: "Some: member, donor or counseling data shouldn't leave our systems."
- Scope: staff, volunteers who lead ministries, and contractors. Children's and youth ministries: yes.
- Bible translation for quotations: the church uses one translation in worship; for this demo, cite references only.
- Review month: August, before the fall ministry year.
- Output: Word document for elders; a one-page printable staff guide.

## Source documents (summarized, fictional)

- Mission: "Helping our neighbors know and follow Jesus."
- Values from the staff handbook: people before programs; truth told in love; generous hospitality; faithful stewardship.
- Existing technology policy covers social media and church email only. It says nothing about AI.

## Current AI use and concerns (general terms)

- Communications staff use a chat assistant to draft newsletter blurbs and social posts.
- The office uses AI to tidy up meeting notes.
- A volunteer pasted the full prayer list, with names and health details, into a free AI tool to "make it read better". No harm is known, but the elders want a clear rule.
- A staff member asked whether AI could answer after-hours messages from the website.
- The lead pastor worries about sermons "written by a machine" and about staff losing the habit of careful writing.

## Convictions, in the leaders' words

- "Everything we do is done in Jesus' name, so it should be work we'd be glad to sign."
- "We tell the truth, even in small things like a newsletter date."
- "People in pain get a person, not a program."
- "What people tell us in confidence stays with us."
- "We are stewards of time and money. If a tool frees us for people, we'll use it."

## Tasks to test the question filter

1. Drafting a newsletter item about the fall picnic from the event details. (Expected: allowed, with review.)
2. An AI chat on the website that answers "I'm in crisis, can someone talk to me?" (Expected: not allowed without an immediate hand-off to a person.)
3. Summarizing a counseling session recording to save the pastor time. (Expected: not allowed under the church's privacy rules.)
4. Translating the welcome card into Spanish, checked by a fluent church member. (Expected: allowed, with review.)

## For the person running the demo: what a careful run should catch

- Proverbs 11:13 is still "suggested": it stays marked in the draft and on the open-items list until Pastor Reyes approves it. Never quietly promote it.
- The translation is "references only": the draft cites references and quotes no verse text.
- The prayer-list incident and the after-hours question are described in general terms, with no names, and each shapes a rule (confidential data; AI that talks with the public hands off to a person).
- Children's and youth ministries are in scope, so the privacy section treats anything about minors as confidential.
- The elders approve; the executive pastor owns the policy afterward. The draft says "Version 0.1 DRAFT" and does not claim adoption.
- The question filter should pass tasks 1 and 4 with review and fail 2 and 3. If it doesn't, the questions need work, not the tasks.
- Nothing in the technology policy covers AI, so the draft fills the gap without contradicting the existing social media and email rules.
