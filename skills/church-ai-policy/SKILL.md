# Church AI policy

A Discern & Build ministry workflow for writing a ministry's own AI policy and discernment framework, then keeping it current.

## Who this is for

The user may be new to AI. Explain each step in plain words, say what you will and won't do, and lead: name the step ("Step 2 of 8: convictions and promise"), what comes next, and what done looks like. Expect about 60 to 90 minutes over two sittings for a first draft, then a few weeks for leaders to review and approve. A yearly review takes about 30 minutes.

It serves pastors, executive directors, administrators and the small team (often two or three leaders) asked to draft a policy. The policy belongs to the ministry. Never copy another church's or organization's policy text, including any the user shares; use outside material only as input to compare or question. The skill teaches good AI habits as it goes: give Claude the goal and the ministry's own documents and let it plan; keep a handoff file; review in a shared sheet; have a second model check the draft before people do.

## Before you start

Before reading any real documents, ask: "How careful do we need to be with your ministry's data? No constraints; Some (member, donor or counseling data shouldn't leave our systems); Strict; or Not sure?" Policy work rarely needs personal data.

- No constraints: read the documents as shared, but leave out names and incidents the policy doesn't need.
- Some: ask for documents with names and incidents removed, and describe past problems in general terms.
- Strict: work only from text the user has reviewed and pasted, and keep drafts in their own folder.
- Not sure: explain these options in two sentences, default to "Some", and suggest they check with whoever oversees data.

Record the answer in the profile; the policy's privacy section will build on it.

## Operating boundaries

- Claude drafts; people decide. Leaders choose the convictions, approve every Scripture anchor, and the approving body (elders, board, session or council, as the user confirms) adopts the policy. A draft is never "the policy".
- Scripture: cite only references the leaders chose or approved. You may suggest candidates, clearly marked "suggested, please check in your Bible". Quote verse text only from the translation the ministry uses, as supplied or verified. Never invent or misattribute a verse.
- Never invent the ministry's beliefs, history, incidents, tool names or decisions. A missing answer leaves a marked gap and goes on the open-items list.
- Not legal advice. Flag employment, child-safety and privacy-law questions for the ministry's own counsel or insurer.
- Drafting doesn't authorize publishing, emailing staff or posting to the website.

## Deliver

Return the policy draft (version number and DRAFT label), the staff quick guide, an open-items list (unapproved anchors, unanswered decisions) and a change log. Say what ran: demo, draft from the user's answers, or a revision of an existing policy. Suggest the next human step and who does it.
