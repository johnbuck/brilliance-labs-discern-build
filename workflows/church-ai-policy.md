# Write your church's AI policy

## What this helps you do

Write your own Christ-centered AI policy, grounded in your convictions and the Scripture your leaders choose. You finish with a 2 to 4 page draft for your elders or board and a one-page guide for staff and volunteers.

## Who it's for

Pastors, directors and administrators asked to draft a policy. No technical background needed.

## What you'll need

- An AI assistant such as Claude; Claude Desktop can read documents from your folders.
- Your statement of faith, mission or values, and any staff handbook.
- About 60 to 90 minutes over two sittings, then a few weeks for leaders to review.

## The steps

1. Tell the AI your tradition and who approves policies, and share your core documents.
2. Put your convictions about work, truth, people and technology into your own words.
3. Turn those into 4 to 6 principles, each anchored in a passage your pastor chooses and checks in context.
4. Write 3 to 5 questions anyone can ask before using AI, and test them on real tasks.
5. Decide what stays human (pastoral care, counseling, decisions about people), what a person reviews before it goes out in your name, and how freely any AI tool may act on its own.
6. Add staff and volunteer guidelines, privacy rules, and when to say AI helped.
7. Have a fresh AI chat audit the draft, then review it together in a shared comment sheet.
8. Your elders or board adopt it as version 1.0. Review it yearly.

## What stays with people

Your beliefs, every Scripture reference, the final wording, adoption and publishing. The AI drafts and asks questions; your leaders decide.

## Keep it safe

A policy needs no personal information. Leave out names when describing past problems. This is not legal advice; ask your counsel or insurer about employment and child-safety wording.

## Try it

Copy this into any AI chat:

> I lead [church or ministry name], part of the [tradition] tradition. Help me write our own AI policy, not a copy of anyone else's. Ask one question at a time. Start with our convictions, then draft 4 to 6 principles. For each, ask which Bible passage anchors it for us; if you suggest one, mark it "suggested" and never quote verse text I haven't given you. Then build a 3 to 5 question filter, rules for what stays human, staff guidelines, privacy and disclosure, and a yearly review. Label everything DRAFT.
