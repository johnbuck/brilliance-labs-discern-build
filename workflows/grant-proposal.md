# Grant proposal

## What this helps you do

Decide whether a foundation is worth applying to, then write a letter of intent (LOI, a short first application) or full proposal with AI as your drafting partner. Like counting the cost before building a tower (Luke 14:28), an honest early "no" saves weeks.

## Who it's for

Executive directors, pastors, development staff and volunteers who write grant applications, including first-timers.

## What you'll need

- The funder's guidelines or request for proposals (RFP), pasted or as a link
- Past proposals, annual report, budget, board list, financial statements, program numbers
- Your treasurer for the budget
- About 45 minutes to set up and check fit, then two or three sessions for an LOI

## The steps

1. Have the AI list every question, word limit, deadline and attachment in the funder's guidelines.
2. Check fit (mission, eligibility, deadline, capacity, amount). A person decides go or no-go.
3. Build a fact sheet where every number has a source and a date.
4. Draft the short form fields first, then each answer in plain paragraphs under its limit, ready to paste into the online form.
5. Build the budget from your treasurer's numbers, with a sentence explaining each line.
6. Review in rounds: a second AI chat grades the draft against the guidelines, then your reviewers comment. Log every comment and save numbered versions (v1, v2, v3).
7. Walk the final checklist. You submit.
8. Put report due dates on a calendar with an owner for each; if awarded, your treasurer tracks the grant as a restricted gift.

## What stays with people

Whether to apply, the final budget, every claim about results and partners, and pressing submit. A named person, often the executive director with the treasurer, approves.

## Keep it safe

Leave out donor names, individual salaries and personal stories unless truly needed. Never let the AI guess at results, numbers or partners; missing facts should show as [TO CONFIRM]. Don't share portal passwords with any AI.
