# Annual budget

## What this helps you do

Plan next year's operating budget with your leaders and treasurer, build it as a spreadsheet, and bring it to the board with a one-page memo that says exactly what the board is approving.

## Who it's for

Pastors, executive directors, treasurers, finance committees and administrators who prepare the budget for a board vote.

## What you'll need

- Last year's income and expenses by month, and this year's so far (QuickBooks Online "Profit and Loss by Month").
- This year's budget and any known changes.
- A treasurer or finance committee to review.
- Time: an hour to plan, a 2 to 3 hour working session to build, and a few short review rounds.

## The steps

1. Talk the year through with Claude first: goals, changes, worries and the date of the vote. No spreadsheet yet.
2. Gather last year's actuals, this year's actuals so far, and a forecast for the rest of this year.
3. List every assumption on its own line, with its source and who decides it. Staff pay stays a labeled placeholder until the board or its committee sets it.
4. Claude builds the workbook: budget by line, a month-by-month cash view, and downside, base and stretch scenarios.
5. Check the lowest cash month in each scenario and the giving level where the year breaks even.
6. Claude writes a one-page board memo: what the board approves, kept separate from the assumptions behind it.
7. The treasurer asks questions; Claude answers each with the numbers and updates the workbook until it's ready for the vote.

## What stays with people

Every assumption, all compensation, and the approval itself. The board decides the spending plan, any fundraising goal, the most that may be drawn from reserves (a safety net, not planned income), and the minimum cash balance to keep.

## Keep it safe

Use summary reports, not donor or payroll detail. Show pay as totals in the memo. Never share passwords. Before drawing on a donor-restricted endowment, check the gift terms with a CPA or attorney.

## Try it

Copy this into any AI chat, then attach or paste your numbers:

> I'm preparing our ministry's budget for next fiscal year for a board vote. Before building anything, ask me one question at a time about our goals, changes and calendar. Then list assumptions in a table with source and who decides each, keeping staff pay as labeled placeholders. Finally, draft a one-page board memo that separates what the board approves from the assumptions.
